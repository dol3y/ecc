Sunday 8th February: The City and Greenwich


On Tower Bridge
Photo: Bharat

On the bus (1)
Photo: Cathy

On the bus (2)
Photo: Cathy

Eleven riders assembled at Edmonton Leisure Centre hoping for a dry day. We followed the rather twisting C1 cycleway through Tottenham, Stamford Hill, Stoke Newington and Hoxton to the fringe of the City. From here on the traffic, which had been quite light, became more of an obstacle. Several times the group was split by traffic lights as we forged our way past the Bank of England and the Tower of London to reach London’s most iconic bridge, Tower Bridge. A brief stop for photos on the bridge before we hit the south bank and followed a relatively new segregated cycle path all the way to Greenwich. At Greenwich we stopped for lunch, and several of us discovered the the street food delights on sale at Greenwich Market. Fortified, we continued around the back streets of Greenwich Peninsula until we rather suddenly came upon the southern terminus of the Silvertown Tunnel Cycle Shuttle Bus. This will carry up to eight riders plus bikes through the tunnel to Royal Victoria. As there were eleven of us we had to split into two groups on successive buses before we regrouped at the northern end. From here we rode through the Olympic Park and Walthamstow Wetlands (where we sheltered for about 15 minutes from a short but heavy shower) to reach Edmonton about 3:00 pm.
Leader: Peter; Riders Bridget, Cassiano, Cathy, Deirdre, Geoff, Pat, Paul, Richard, Stephen; Backstop Bharat.

Sunday 15h March: The Magical Mystery History Tour

A ride with a difference, possible a first for the club: a ride around north London visiting blue plaques. Setting off from Coffee Break, our route took us via the one blue plaque in Palmers Green (Stevie Smith) to Hampstead, then onto Primrose Hill, Camden and home vie Stroud Green. Sunday 22nd March: Queenswood Café:


The Garden at Queenswood Café
Photo: Angie

Ready to leave the café
Photo: Angie

This short ride took place on the first really sunny and warm spring Sunday morning of the year, departing from the (still closed!) Enfield Town Park Café. Eleven cyclists were joined by two more at Winchmore Hill station. The riders warmed up, ascending Old Park Ave towards Winchmore Hill, admiring the houses on Broad Walk, and, staying mostly on quiet residential roads, the group moved on towards Palmers Green and down the familiar routes of Palmerston and Western Road, before cycling through more residential streets, reaching Hornsey. However, this time, Wood Vale and Queen's Wood were swapped for Wolseley Road and Shepherds Hill, which felt less challenging at first, but the last bit, the footpath from Highgate Tube Station to Wood Lane, turned out to be steep and needed effort. The group very much enjoyed their cakes in the picturesque and tranquil kitchen gardens of Queen's Wood Café, which we had to ourselves. The way home led down Muswell Hill, past Cranley Gardens, Alexandra Palace, Nightingale Gardens, Palmerston Road and Firs Lane, where the ride ended.
Leader: Angie; Riders: Bharat, Bridget, Cassiano, Dave, Debbie, Geoff, Peter, Richard, Sarah, Sue; Backstop Jeff

Sunday 5th April: Hatfield House

Nine of us assembled at Oakwood Park during an unexpected downpour. With the forecast assuring us that this wouldn't last we set off anyway and the rain immediately stopped as we made our way out of the park We headed towards Potters Bar, Welham Green and then on to Hatfield House. Here we met with an officious steward who told us we weren't allowed to bring our bikes into the stable yard. We told him that we have done this on numerous occasions in the past and locked them up safely where they are not causing any obstruction to others. However he was insistent that we move our bikes to the bike shed in the car park so we did as we were told, even though the shed didn't look like the securest of places to leave expensive bikes. After a leisurely lunch in the café we were relieved to find our bikes still in the shed and we set off for home. After retracing our steps to Welham Green we headed towards Brookmans Park, circled around Potters Bar and then on to Hadley Wood. We arrived back at Cockfosters where we said our goodbyes and went our separate ways.
Leader: Geoff; Riders: Angie, Imran, Pat (part ride), Peter M, Richard N, Stephen, Tim; Backstop Cathy

Sunday 10th May: Elstree Aerodrome

Following the scorching sunshine of the previous day, some of us arrived at Oakwood Park slightly underdressed for what was quite a chilly morning. With one rider having suffered a puncture on the way to the start we arranged to meet him enroute and set off towards Oakwood Underground before making our way along Cockfosters Road to Beech Hill. After a short stop for water at the top of Camlet Way we continued into Barnet and then down Galley Lane to the Horse Bridge. From here we made our way through Ridge and followed the country lanes to Shenley. After passing the Orchard Cafe we continued downhill to Radlett and then up Loom Lane and onwards past Aldenham School and to the Aerodrome. The cold weather had encouraged us to ride at a faster pace than is usual for us and we arrived a bit earlier than planned. The cafe was fairly busy but we found tables with a view of the runway and enjoyed our various lunch choices. Coming outside again the temperature seemed to have dropped even lower and, although there had been one or two murmurings about the number of hills on the way there, on the way back I think most of us were grateful of a few inclines to keep us warm. The return journey saw us pass through Elstree, down Mays Lane, on to East Barnet, through Oakhill Park, up Osidge Lane and back to Oakwood Park.
Leader Geoff; Riders Dave P, Mike, Richard N, Tom, Virginia; Backstop Peter M

Sunday 17th May: Codicote

We met at Trent Park all psyched up for a long ride and hoping we would miss the forecast afternoon showers. Riding out on Cockfosters and Waggon Roads, we faced our first decision when Dancers Hill Road was closed. But it looked passable by bike and indeed this was the case, as the holes only covered half of the road. Not sure what was going on, as it is only about a year ago this road was closed for months. We continued through Potters Bar and onto the A1000 to Hatfield. Not a lot of fun riding on a busy main road but it meant we reached Hatfield and puncture number 1 in good time. This fixed, we continued to Welwyn Garden City for a short break, and from here the ride became more interesting. The loop north of WGC took us past the Arts and Crafts church at Ayot St Peter and then into Codicote itself. This stretch was enlivened by a deer crossing the road and a red kite taking off just in front of us. A short (and steep) way out of Codicode took us to Spokes cyclists café where we stopped for various toasted delights including some very overcooked poached eggs. We returned through Woolmer Green, Tewin, Welwyn Garden City (puncture No 2), Hatfield then back onto the A1000 before diverting off to the start of Coopers Lane Road where most riders left for the Enfield area, whilst two others rode on to Barnet. A long ride of about 47 miles but very satisfying, and the showers held off.
Leader Peter; Riders Bridget, Cass, Geoff, Richard, Tim, Tom; Backstops Jeff, Richard.

Sunday 14th June: Les Bailey Memorial Ride 1: Richmond Park


Ready for the Off
Photo: Geoff

After a group photo seventeen of us set off from the Little Green Dragon and met up with our eighteenth team member at Arnos Park. With the weather forecast good we were in good spirits as we took an urban route to Richmond Park via Finchley, Hampstead Garden Suburb, Golders Green, Cricklewood, Acton and Chiswick.  On arriving at the Park we cycled halfway round to Pembroke Lodge where we stopped for lunch. In spite of a large queue at the café it didn’t take us too long to get served and we enjoyed our food in the sun at the tables outside. This wasn’t a difficult ride but the toughest of the hills proved to be in the park itself.  We encountered another challenging hill on our way back to the Roehampton Gate exit after which it was mostly plain sailing back to the start. We crossed the Thames at Putney Bridge before heading towards South Kensington and through Hyde Park to Marble Arch.  As we approached Regents Park we were forced to walk our bikes for a short while and take a slight detour to reach the York Gate entrance. This was due to road closures for a temporary street fair which hadn’t been present on the recces. We made a comfort stop at the entrance to the Broad Walk path in Regents Park where, in spite of a daunting queue, one of our team was unable to resist the temptation of purchasing and consuming an ice cream. This slightly delayed our return but we made good time for the rest of the ride through Camden, Hornsey, Wood Green and back to the Little Green Dragon. Here we were given a warm welcome and treated to plenty of delicious food and drink.
Leader Geoff; Riders Andrew, Angie, Bharat, Bridget, Cliff N, Dave P, Deirdre, Hal, Hassan, Imran, Jeff, Julian, Keeley, Richard N, Tim, Tom; Backstop Peter M

Sunday 12th July: Dobbs Weir

With the weather still warm a large group of us assembled at Beggars Hollow and set off towards Forty Hall and the A10 cycle path. We sadly came across a couple of, presumably, homeless people asleep in the underpass who appeared to have set up home there. They had thoughtfully left room for us to cycle through on our bikes. After passing Cedars Park we turned down Theobalds Lane and crossed back over the A10 using the footbridge. It wasn’t long before we encountered the first of a very small number of hills, which took us up Park Lane and Park Lane Paradise. Whilst some of us relished this challenge, there were a few mutterings heard from others. After this, though, we could enjoy cruising down Holy Cross Hill and travelling through country lanes up to White Stubbs Lane and down Baas Hill. From here we took the side streets to Broxbourne High Road, down Mill Lane and through to the Old Nazing Road. As we approached our destination we had to take a short detour due to overhead cable work but we arrived at Dobbs Weir in good time. Here we enjoyed an extended lunch break with some choosing to eat in the café while others plumped for a liquid lunch in the pub. On the way back we cycled through Lea Valley Park with Clayton Hill being the only incline of any note. We stopped for ice creams at Fishers Green Lane, and from Waltham Abbey we headed for Gunpowder Park and Enfield Island village before cycling through Albany Park and Enfield Crematorium on our way back to Beggars Hollow. A few of us stayed to listen to the concert (60s music by the Soul Agents) taking place at Hilly Fields.
Leader Geoff; Riders Bernie, Bharat, Dave P, Harvey, Jayne, Jeff, Jen, Karl, Maria, Michael G, Pat, Paul L, Peter M, Rosa, Sarah C, Sarah M, Sue, Tom; Backstop Cathy C

Sunday 26th July: Bricket Wood


"Stepping Stones across the Ver"
Photo: Tim

After weeks without rain the first drops fell about 15 minutes before the start. But we thought it would only be a shower, so we delayed for 10-15 minutes and indeed rain did stop. There were some threatening looking clouds at times during the ride but rain did indeed hold off for the rest of the day. Bad news for gardeners! We set off along a familiar route of Waggon Road, Trotters Bottom and Black Lion Hill before turning before crossing the M25 to Colney Street and Smug Oak Lane. We were a little ahead of schedule, and as the pub at the planned stop was not yet open we stopped for a photo opportunity at some stepping stones across the River Ver. On to The Gate pub where were made very welcome, although the menu and beer options left something to be desired. After lunch we continued through Bricket Wood and past the Picture House where we stopped to admire the terracotta reliefs. Continuing we skirted the edge of Watford, through the grounds of Wall Hall, across roadwords at Letchmore Heath before finishing through Radlett, Borehamwood and Barnet.

Sunday 23rd August: London Equestrian Centre:

We started off along a familiar route of Cockfosters Road, Waggon Road and Trotters Bottom to the A1 horsebridge, then past the Mops and Brooms pub to Borehamwood. Here we took the backstreets before ending up by the station, then over the railway bridge and up the long hill to Barnet Lane. A short delay for an errant chain, then on to Edgewarebury Lane. This drops steeply down to a rural oasis in the centre of Barnet, but the ostensibly paved surface can only be described as atrocious! Safely negotiated, we rode on the cycle path alongside Edgware Way to Apex Corner, which fortunately has a cyclists and pedestrians underpass. Up the steep hill through the grounds of Mill Hill School and along Partingdale Lane took us to the London Equestrian Centre, where last year we had enjoyed lunch in the stables cafe. But this tine the cafe was closed – we had half expected it to be as it had been closed on the recce ride. A stable hand told us that this was because the chef was unwell – if so we wish him or her well and hope that the next time we pass full service will be restored. Instead we continued to Woodside Park where we found a very accommodating cafe, although they had sold out of chocolate croissants. After a refreshment break we rode back to Oak Hill Park then on to our separate homeward destinations.
Leader: Peter; Riders Andrew, Chris, Geoff, Imran, Paul; Backstop Bharat.

Sunday 6th September: Ridgeway Loop

We gathered at the Beggars Hollow café ready for a short, half day ride but one that promised a variety of terrain. Setting off initially through the abandoned Whitewebbs golf course, we passed through Crews Hill before reaching Coopers Lane Road towards Potters Bar. Sunday 27th September: Harlow Park

Nine of us set off from Whitewebbs Cafe on a chilly, but dry, morning. Passing through Enfield Crematorium and along Ordnance Road we made our way to Gunpowder Park. Soon after cycling through Waltham Abbey we encountered Horseshoe Hill closely followed by Crown Hill after which we were very nicely warmed up. We continued to Epping Uplands and along Epping Road to the outskirts of Harlow. On arriving at the Park we found the cafe area extremely busy and occupied by a large number of greyhounds. Apparently, we had chosen the day of the Great Greyhound Global Walk for our ride! We joined the long queue for food and drink and managed to squeeze ourselves in amongst the greyhounds and their owners to enjoy our lunch outside. I must say the greyhounds were very well behaved, probably better than some of us. On the way back we passed Roydon and cycled through the Lea Valley Park to Cheshunt. We finished the ride with a short spell on the New River Path before going our separate ways near to where we started.
Leader: Geoff; Riders: Andrew D, Bharat, Deirdre, Jeff, Peter M, Richard, Tom; Backstop Cathy C

Sunday 4th October: Broxbourne via New River Path

Today's ride was another first for the club...a ride along the New River Path all the way to Broxbourne. We set off from Enfield Town Park up to the start of the Enfield section of the New River Path in Tenniswood Rd and cycled north. Rather unexpectedly the water level was unusually high and there was even flooding of the path at one point, but we were able to cycle through. On reaching Bullsmore Lane, we went onto the unexplored section of the path. At first it was quite narrow and it was clear that this section was incomplete. However as we crossed the M25 the path widened and we continued. The remainder of the path varied in width and surface, and although it took us through very nice countryside, it was annoying to discover various kissing gates which slowed down our progress. We reached our stopping point, the Old Mill Retreat about 11 and enjoyed a drink and a snack before beginning our return. The route back took us along the River Lea tow path, around the bird sanctuary and onto Waltham Abbey. From there we cycled through gunpowder park, across to the A10 and back down the new river path to Enfield.
Ride leader: Tim; Riders: Paul, Richard, Bharat, Colin, Bridget, Caroline, Angie, Deirdre, Cathy, Honorata; Backstop: Peter;
